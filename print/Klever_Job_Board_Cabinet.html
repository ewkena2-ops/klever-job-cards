THE CARD LEANS BACK

This is what makes 1090 work.

42° OFF VERTICAL

297 becomes 220. Standing straight up, an A4 card needs 297 of height and there is not that much left once four box files are in the bay. Leaning it back at 42° costs 220 and the job code still faces you.

100 CHARCOAL LIP

One strip per bay. The cards rest against it, the live one on top. About twelve fit in the pocket behind it. Lift the front one and the next is already facing you.

FIVE THINGS THAT MATTER

  1. The top is the work surface — band it on three edges and use a full 3180 piece, not a joint. It is the one part people will touch every day.
  2. 213 clear per bay is the whole design. An A4 card is 210 — cut the dividers on a stop block so no bay ends up 208.
  3. Both shelves carry box weight. Screw into every divider, not just the ends.
  4. Level the plinth before anything else. A 3.18 m cabinet shows a twisted floor immediately.
  5. Two anti-tip brackets into the wall. Loaded with 56 box files this carries about 140 kg of paper.

Standing room: leave 900 in front of it. People will be pulling A4 boxes out at 400 and reading cards at 1000 in the same movement.
